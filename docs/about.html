About

An independent fan project, built to be genuinely useful

Anime Array was built because the tools anime fans actually reach for were optimised for engagement rather than for helping anyone decide what to watch or find someone to talk to about it.

The short version

Anime Array is a free, independent, fan-built platform of AI tools for anime fans. It hosts no video, requires no account for any core feature, is funded entirely by clearly labelled advertising, and does not sell user data. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by or licensed by any anime studio, publisher, licensor or rights holder.

Four products sit under one roof: character roleplay, the Otaku AI assistant, AniPick AI recommendations, and Vision AI image generation. All four are indexed at the AI Hub.

The one-sentence description

Anime Array is a free, independent, fan-built platform of AI tools for anime fans — character roleplay, a conversational anime assistant, a recommendation engine and an image generator — requiring no account and funded by clearly labelled advertising.

Why it was built

Anime recommendation looks like a solved problem. It is not, and the reason is that most of what is available was not built to solve it.

Ask a recommendation system what you want to watch and the answer is nearly always the same short list of commercially popular titles, ranked by whatever keeps you on the platform. That is a coherent goal — but it is not the same goal as helping you find something worth two hours of your evening. Where a tool does try to learn from your history, it usually needs an account, a login, and enough tracked viewing data before it will say anything useful. Ask a stranger for their watchlist and you get ten titles, four of which they have already seen, and the system cannot tell the difference.

Anime Array takes the opposite position. Start with the smallest input that carries real signal — what you watched, what you loved, what you dropped — and build outward from there. No account. No warm-up period. Something useful within one sitting.

The second motivation was simpler to state. Anime fan spaces tend to be excellent at archives, wikis and lore databases, and thin on anything that feels like a place rather than a reference. Anime Array was built to be a place. Somewhere you can actually talk to Gojo about his students, tell Sukuna he is being exactly as insufferable as he thinks, ask Levi a direct question and get a short flat answer, or argue with Vegeta about pride. The characters are written to behave differently under identical pressure, because a platform that gives you twelve characters with one voice is not a roleplay product.

Who runs it

Anime Array is run by a small independent team with direct access to the codebase and the server. There is no management layer between the people who build the platform and the people who decide what gets changed on it, which is the reason decisions on this site tend to be made quickly and explained plainly rather than filtered through a roadmap nobody outside the company can read.

The practical consequence is that when something breaks, the person who can fix it already understands how it works. There is no handoff, no translation layer, and no queue of tickets. This matters more than it sounds — a small site run by people who do not understand their own stack fails slowly and confusingly, and a large one run by people who do understand it fails quickly and obviously.

Direct contact is available through the contact form or at contact@animearray.site. Messages are read and answered by a person. Corrections are treated as the highest-priority kind of message, because a platform built on lore is only worth anything if the lore is right.

How it is funded

Advertising, and nothing else. Every ad that appears on the platform is labelled as an advertisement, and the funding model is stated plainly rather than buried. There is no subscription, no premium tier, no paid unlock on a core feature, and no sale of user data to anyone — not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to anyone.

This is a deliberate trade. Advertising is a blunt instrument: it pays the bills and it also introduces third-party code onto pages that would otherwise be clean. The compromise Anime Array makes is to keep the number of advertising surfaces low, keep them clearly separated from the product itself, and make sure that every core function still works with advertising blocked entirely. If you block every ad on Anime Array, you lose nothing that matters.

The rules the project runs on

These are in priority order, because priorities are what actually resolve disagreements about what to build.

  1. It has to work on a slow phone on mobile data. Every core feature is tested on a throttled connection before it ships. If a feature needs a good connection to be usable, it does not ship.
  2. No account to use the core. Anything requiring registration sits alongside the free, no-signup experience rather than replacing it. Accounts are a convenience, not a toll gate.
  3. Readable over clever. A page that is easy to read and easy to repair is worth more than one that scores marginally better on a metric nobody checks.
  4. Honest defaults. If a feature can mislead someone about what this site is or how it makes money, that is a defect regardless of how well the feature itself performs.
  5. Reversible changes. New work should be possible to undo in a single step. This is not a nice-to-have aspiration; it is the reason the platform has never been down for long.
  6. Performance is a design decision rather than something recovered afterwards by optimisation.
  7. Accuracy outranks fluency. Where a character is given a question canon does not cover, the reply is reasoned from that character's temperament and stated as such — never presented as a quote from the source material.

Intellectual property and non-affiliation

This section is deliberately blunt because vagueness here causes real problems for real people.

Anime Array is an independent fan project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, licensed by, or connected to any anime studio, publisher, licensor, broadcaster, distributor or rights holder. Nobody has approved this project. Nobody has reviewed the characters. Claiming otherwise would be a lie with legal consequences, and it is not a lie Anime Array tells.

All character names, series titles and related intellectual property belong to their respective owners. Anime, manga, voice performances and any other protected media are not reproduced on this platform. The characters are original AI systems inspired by existing anime characters; each is a conversational simulation with its own written personality, not a copy of any performance and not a recreation of any copyrighted text. No animation is shown. No manga panel is shown. No episode, film, audio track or subtitle is hosted, uploaded, mirrored or linked for download anywhere on the platform.

Where a character's dialogue could be mistaken for a quotation from the source material, that is a limitation Anime Array acknowledges openly rather than tries to obscure: these are original conversational responses written in a character's voice, and they are not canon. Full detail is in the policies page and in the live site's editorial and corrections policy.

What Anime Array will never do

Some boundaries are worth stating in public so that nobody has to take them on trust.

  • Host or mirror video. No episodes, films, subtitles or media files. The platform points people to legitimate services; it does not attempt to replace them.
  • Claim an affiliation that does not exist. No studio, publisher, licensor, network or distributor has endorsed, licensed, sponsored or approved this project.
  • Clone voices. No character's dialogue is spoken by a real person in real time, and no voice actor's performance is reproduced anywhere on the platform.
  • Sell or broker personal data. Advertising pays for the site. It does not purchase anything about the people who use it.
  • Charge for a core feature. A paywall behind something basic would contradict the entire premise of the project.
  • Use dark patterns. No fake download buttons, no fake play buttons, no countdown timers, no misleading "click here to download" prompts, no fake urgency anywhere.

Copyright concerns and takedown requests are handled through the contact form and are treated as a priority over anything else in the queue.

Accessibility as a requirement

On Anime Array, accessibility is treated as a requirement rather than a feature, and none of it is conditional on having an account or on any commercial relationship with the site.

Semantic HTML is used so that headings describe a genuine hierarchy rather than being chosen for their size, and controls are identified by their actual role rather than by appearance. Every interactive element is reachable and operable by keyboard alone, with a visible focus indicator that does not depend on colour to be seen. Text meets or exceeds WCAG AA contrast ratios against the backgrounds it is actually rendered on, which is a stricter test than checking a palette on paper. Every meaningful image carries descriptive alternative text, and decorative images are marked as decorative so assistive technology skips them rather than announcing noise. Form fields have labels that make sense read aloud, and validation errors are explained in words rather than indicated by a colour change alone. Status changes and streaming responses are announced through live regions so a screen reader user is not left guessing whether anything happened. The prefers-reduced-motion preference is honoured for transitions, animated backgrounds and scroll behaviour.

The reason this is stated at length: a free site asking for trust has an obligation to be usable by the people paying it, and accessibility work that is only claimed rather than verified is worse than none at all.

A note on how it is built

The platform is a self-hosted, server-rendered application. Pages arrive complete from the server, core reading and character chat work without JavaScript, third-party services are called server-side so credentials never reach the browser, and configuration is applied at render time so changes take effect immediately and can be reverted in a single step. There is no single-page application shell and no client-side framework dependency for rendering content.

Operational detail is not published. The technology page describes the approach and the reasoning without documenting internals that would help somebody attack the site.

Getting involved

There is no application process and no approval step. Four kinds of contribution are genuinely useful, and the first is the one most people overlook.

  1. Corrections. If a character's description, a tool description, a series fact or a claim on this site is wrong, that is the most valuable fix available and it requires no technical skill at all. Send it through the contact form.
  2. Translation. Native speakers improving or adding a language. English is current; more is planned, and machine translation is not an acceptable substitute.
  3. Accessibility reports. Anything that blocks a particular assistive technology, screen reader, keyboard user or browser combination.
  4. Character requests. Submitted through the AI Hub. The roster follows what readers actually ask for rather than a fixed release schedule.

Technical contributions follow the same shape as the platform itself: small, reversible, readable, and verified before they land. A change that cannot be undone in one step needs a considerably stronger justification than usual. The process is documented in CONTRIBUTING.md in the repository.

Getting in touch

Questions, corrections, copyright concerns, accessibility reports and character requests are all handled through the same route: the contact form, or email at contact@animearray.site. Security issues have a separate, private route described in SECURITY.md and should not be filed as public issues.

The most common questions are answered on the FAQ page. If the answer is not there, that is a gap worth telling Anime Array about.
