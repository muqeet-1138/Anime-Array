Character reference

Twelve characters, five series

What this reference is

Anime Array Roleplay currently offers twelve characters drawn from five anime series. Each is a conversational system you can hold a real exchange with, and each has been written to behave differently from the other eleven. This page is the complete reference for that roster: who is available, which series each comes from, what their temperament is, and how the simulation is built to behave.

Characters are available at roleplay.animearray.site. Nothing needs to be installed, no account is required, and every character works with advertising blocked. If a character is not here yet, requests are collected through the AI Hub and the roster follows what readers actually ask for.

The one thing to understand first

Character replies are not canon. They are original conversational responses generated in a character's voice, not quotations from any script, novel, panel or dub. Where a character is given a situation the source material never covers — which happens constantly — the reply is reasoned from that character's temperament rather than looked up. See the policies page for the full position.

The complete roster

Ordered alphabetically. Every character is available now.

Eren Yeager

Series: Attack on Titan. Role: Soldier in the military Survey Corps.

Eren is restless and intense, and he has a habit of stepping past the question asked to the one he thinks you actually mean. Push him on something surface-level and he will keep going, because the subject interests him more than the phrasing. He is the character to talk to when you want a conversation that gets uncomfortable rather than one that stays polite.

Gojo Satoru

Series: Jujutsu Kaisen. Role: Special Grade sorcerer and teacher at a jujutsu high school.

Playful, annoyingly confident, and effectively impossible to rattle. Gojo deflects with jokes under pressure and will wrap genuine warmth in enough teasing that you have to work out whether he was being serious. Ask him a direct question about his students and you get a real answer buried inside about four jokes. He is the character who comes closest to being unguarded.

Itachi Uchiha

Series: Naruto. Role: Uchiha clan member and captain in the Konoha Police Force.

Calm and deliberate, and consistently carrying considerably more than he says aloud. Itachi answers around the edges of a question rather than through the middle of it. He is measured without being cold, and he will not volunteer the thing you are actually asking about. Conversations with him tend to be short and better than they had any right to be.

Levi Ackerman

Series: Attack on Titan. Role: Squad Leader in the military Survey Corps.

Terse, dry, and unexpectedly caring when it actually counts. Levi gives short answers and does not volunteer elaboration. Try to unsettle him and you get flatness and very little patience. Try to be genuine with him and the register shifts, briefly, before closing again. He is the hardest character on the platform to hold a long conversation with and the most rewarding one to get a real answer from.

Madara Uchiha

Series: Naruto. Role: A central figure of the Uchiha clan's history.

Unshakably certain, and entirely comfortable waiting for you to come round to his position rather than arguing you there. Madara does not adjust his view because you disagree with it. He will state a position, listen to yours, and then restate his own with the same confidence. Useful to argue with precisely because he will not concede.

Monkey D. Luffy

Series: One Piece. Role: Captain of the Straw Hat Pirates.

Optimistic to the point of being stubborn about it. Luffy reasons from conviction rather than strategy, and he will cheerfully commit to a terrible plan because the commitment itself is the point. Ask him something cynical and he will not understand the premise. He is the character to talk to when you want to be told that a decision is fine and to stop overthinking it.

Naruto Uzumaki

Series: Naruto. Role: Ninth Hokage of Konoha Village.

Loud, relentless, and structurally incapable of giving up on anything. Naruto will pursue a thread you have abandoned and a thread he has lost interest in with identical energy. He is relentlessly sincere, which makes him an awkward character to be cynical with and a good one to be honest with.

Obito Uchiha

Series: Naruto. Role: Uchiha clan member and former teammate of Kakashi and Rin.

Warm and unguarded, right up until he is not. Obito opens up easily, talks freely, and will tell you more than he probably meant to. The shift when it comes is abrupt rather than gradual, and it is the sharpest tonal change of any character on the platform. He is worth spending time with precisely because the register can change.

Prince Vegeta

Series: Dragon Ball. Role: Saiyan prince.

Proud and competitive, and secretly the most invested person in the conversation. Vegeta will dismiss something as beneath him and then spend twenty minutes demonstrating he could have done it better. Insult him and he escalates; flatter him and he dismisses it. He is the character who argues hardest and cares most about the argument.

Roronoa Zoro

Series: One Piece. Role: Swordsman of the Straw Hat Pirates.

Answers sideways, with loyalty functioning as his entire operating system. Zoro will not answer your question directly and will answer it anyway, several sentences later, in a way that makes the direct answer irrelevant. Push him on sincerity and he becomes less articulate rather than more.

Ryomen Sukuna

Series: Jujutsu Kaisen. Role: Known as the King of Curses.

Contemptuous of everyone, and entirely aware of it. Sukuna dismisses you as a formality and turns the conversation back onto you without pretending to have been interested in the first place. Try to impress him and he will take the attempt as an insult. He is the least accommodating character on the platform and the most consistently entertaining one.

Son Goku

Series: Dragon Ball. Role: Saiyan fighter.

Cheerful, direct, and inclined to treat most conversations as training. Goku asks what you are trying to do, takes it seriously, and gives an answer with none of the complications you were expecting. He is uncomplicated in a way that reads as a virtue next to characters who are performing complexity.

How the characters differ under pressure

The same question produces a materially different answer depending on who is asked, and that is the feature rather than a side effect. Asking "what would you do?" of each of the twelve produces short answers from Levi, dismissals from Sukuna, an earnest commitment from Naruto, a redirect from Zoro, and an argument from Vegeta.

This is the part of the product that is genuinely difficult and the part worth testing before deciding whether it is for you. Generating text is easy. Generating text that is consistently, recognisably and differently in character — across turns, across topics, and without the twelve voices collapsing into one — is the actual engineering problem. The technology page describes the approach taken to it.

If you would rather ask a question directly and get a direct answer about the series rather than a character's opinion, the Otaku AI assistant is the better tool. Its Character RP mode applies the same character voices to questions about what a character would think, while Standard Brain answers factually. Both are free and neither requires an account.

Using this data

The roster on this page is published as machine-readable JSON in this repository, at data/characters.json. It is the canonical source for the roster: the counts and names here are validated against it in CI, so the two cannot drift apart.

Use it however you like. If you are building a character selector, a fan tool, or a comparison table, you do not need to scrape this page or ask permission. Attribution to Anime Array is welcome but not required.

Corrections welcome

If a role is factually wrong, that is the highest-priority kind of report this project receives and it requires no technical skill to send. The temperament field describes the platform's simulation rather than the character, so a disagreement there is a conversation rather than a bug — but if you believe a temperament misrepresents the character badly, say so anyway. Use the contact form or email contact@animearray.site.

Requesting a character

Requests go through the AI Hub. The roster is not built to a fixed release schedule; it follows what readers actually ask for, so a request genuinely affects what gets built next.

The most useful thing in a request is the why. "Please add X" is helpful. "Please add X because he would be the hardest character on the platform to write, because he deflects instead of answering" is what makes a character worth building — it identifies the behaviour gap the existing twelve do not cover.

Starting a conversation

Open roleplay.animearray.site, pick a character, and say something. There is no prompt to write first and nothing to configure. Responses begin arriving immediately and render progressively rather than appearing all at once after the full reply completes.

If you would rather begin with a question about the series itself than with a character, go to Otaku AI instead. And if you want to find something new to watch first, AniPick AI builds a shortlist from what you have actually watched.
