## What Anime Array is

**Anime Array is an independent, fan-built platform of free AI tools for anime fans.** It is a conversational platform, not a streaming service. The site hosts no video of any kind — no episodes, films, or media files — and it is not a broadcaster.

The premise is simple. The hard part of being an anime fan was never finding out what exists. There are already thousands of titles, lists, calendars and trackers. The hard part is *picking what to watch*, and then having someone to talk to about it afterwards. Anime Array is built around that second problem.

The platform has four distinct products, all free and none requiring an account:

- **Anime Array Roleplay** — twelve anime characters you can hold conversations with, each with a distinct voice, temperament and way of speaking
- **Otaku AI** — a conversational anime assistant with two modes and eight built-in tools
- **AniPick AI** — a recommendation engine that builds a shortlist from what you actually watched, loved and skipped
- **Vision AI** — an anime image generator for character art, wallpapers and creative work

It is funded entirely by clearly labelled advertising. No paywall, no upsell, no account required for any core feature, and no selling of user data.

Anime Array is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or connected to any anime studio, publisher, licensor or rights holder. Anime and manga titles and character names belong to their respective owners and are used only to identify the source material each character is inspired by. See [the legal position](#what-we-will-never-do) below.

## Why this exists

Anime recommendation is a solved-looking problem that isn't actually solved.

Ask most recommendation systems what you want to watch and you get the same ten commercially popular titles, ranked by what sells rather than by what you have already seen. They are optimised for engagement, which means they are optimised for keeping you on the platform. That is a different goal from helping you find something worth two hours of your evening.

Where recommendation tools do try to learn from your history, they usually need an account, a login, and enough tracked viewing data before they produce anything useful. Ask a stranger for their watchlist and they give you ten titles, four of which you have already seen, and the system cannot tell the difference.

Anime Array takes the opposite position. Start with the smallest possible input — what you watched, what you loved, what you dropped — and build outward from there. No account required. No history needed before it will say something. And where a recommendation engine is the wrong tool, put a conversation in front of the reader instead, because "tell me something I'd like about this character" is a better question than any filter.

The second motivation was simpler. Anime fan communities tend to be good at archives, wikis and lore databases, and thin on anything that feels like a place rather than a reference. Anime Array was built to be a place — somewhere you can actually talk to the characters, argue with them, ask them what they would do, and get an answer that sounds like them.

## The products

### Anime Array Roleplay

Twelve characters, each with its own voice, temperament and way of speaking.

Most AI chat products produce a single voice with a different name above it. The characters here were written to behave differently under identical pressure:

- **Gojo Satoru** — playful, annoyingly confident, impossible to rattle
- **Ryomen Sukuna** — contemptuous of everyone, and entirely aware of it
- **Naruto Uzumaki** — loud, relentless, incapable of giving up on anything
- **Itachi Uchiha** — calm, deliberate, carrying considerably more than he says aloud
- **Madara Uchiha** — unshakably certain, and entirely comfortable waiting for you to agree
- **Obito Uchiha** — warm and unguarded, right up until he is not
- **Monkey D. Luffy** — optimistic to the point of being stubborn about it
- **Roronoa Zoro** — answers sideways, loyalty as the entire operating system
- **Son Goku** — cheerful, direct, treats most conversations as training
- **Prince Vegeta** — proud and competitive, and secretly the most invested person present
- **Eren Yeager** — restless, intense, inclined to move past the question to the one underneath
- **Levi Ackerman** — terse, dry, and unexpectedly caring when it counts

Try to unsettle Levi and you will get short answers and no patience. Try to impress Sukuna and he will dismiss you and turn the conversation back on you. That difference is the product.

Available at **[roleplay.animearray.site](https://roleplay.animearray.site/)**. New characters are added based on what people actually request, and requests are collected on the [AI Hub](https://animearray.site/ai-hub/).

### Otaku AI

A conversational anime companion — the second core product, and the one that supports everything else.

**Otaku AI runs in two modes:**

- **Standard Brain** — the direct, knowledgeable assistant for questions about series, characters, lore and the platform itself
- **Character RP** — answers in the voice of an anime character instead, useful for exploring how a character would react to a given situation

**Eight tools are built in:**

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| Recommend Anime | A shortlist built from what you tell it you have watched |
| Currently Airing | What is running right now, rather than a stale list |
| Mood Match | Picks something based on how you want to feel, not just genre |
| Anime Search | Finds a title when you only half remember it |
| Character Creator | Build an original character to explore ideas with |
| Anime Quiz | Works out what your taste actually is |
| Power Scaling | Compares characters and their abilities properly |
| Philosophy | The arguments and themes underneath a series |

Conversations can be shared as text, exported as a file, or turned into a shareable image card. History is saved for signed-in users so a thread can be picked up where it was left, and core features require no account at all.

Available at **[otaku.animearray.site](https://otaku.animearray.site/)**.

### AniPick AI

A recommendation engine that works from what you actually watched rather than what is popular.

You provide recent history, your favourites and the titles you dropped. AniPick weighs that specific track record and proposes the next logical step, instead of returning a generic top ten that is identical for every visitor. It is built to be useful within one sitting, before any pattern of behaviour has accumulated.

Available at **[animearray.site/anipick](https://animearray.site/anipick/)**.

### Vision AI

An anime image generator for character art, wallpapers and creative work. It sits alongside the conversational tools so that the same platform covers both talking about anime and making it.
It is scheduled to be published when Anime Array reaches 10,000 monthly users.

Available at **[animearray.site/vision-ai](https://animearray.site/vision-ai/)**.

All four products are indexed together at the **[AI Hub](https://animearray.site/ai-hub/)**.

## How it is built

This section is deliberately high level. The repository exists to explain the project's principles and to host documentation, not to expose its internals. Operational detail lives on the server and is not published here.

The platform is a **self-hosted, server-rendered application**. In broad terms:

- **Server-rendered HTML.** Every page arrives complete from the server. There is no single-page application shell and no dependency on a client-side framework to render content.
- **Progressive enhancement.** Core reading, character chat and the AI assistant work without JavaScript. What JavaScript adds is polish — live streaming of responses, share cards, image generation — never basic access.
- **Server-side integrations.** Third-party AI and advertising calls are made from the server, so API credentials never reach the browser.
- **Render-time configuration.** Behaviour is applied while a page is being produced rather than stored in the content itself, so changes take effect immediately and revert in a single step.
- **Conventional infrastructure.** Standard web-server and database stack, cached at the edge, served over HTTPS.
- **No build step required to read or run.** The published code is the code that runs.

The guiding idea is that a small project cannot afford fragile dependencies. Where a choice existed between something clever and something that could be understood, traced and repaired by one person on a bad evening, the second won almost every time. Further reading is in [`docs/technology.html`](docs/technology.html).

## Design principles

These are the rules we actually apply when deciding what to build. They are in priority order, because priorities are what resolve arguments.

**1. It must work for someone on a slow phone on mobile data.** Every core feature is tested on a throttled connection before it ships. If a feature needs a good connection to be usable, it does not ship.

**2. No account to use the core.** Anything requiring registration sits behind the free, no-signup experience rather than replacing it. Accounts are an opt-in convenience.

**3. Readable over clever.** A page that is easy to read and easy to repair is worth more than one that is marginally better on a metric nobody looks at.

**4. Honest defaults.** If a feature can mislead someone about what the site is or how it makes money, that is a bug regardless of how the feature behaves.

**5. Reversible changes.** New work should be possible to undo in one step. This is not theoretical: it is the reason the platform has never been down for long.

**6. Performance is a design decision, not an optimisation.** Fast pages are a choice made at build time, not something recovered afterwards.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is treated as a requirement rather than a feature, and the following are non-negotiable:

- **Keyboard operation.** Every interactive control is reachable and operable by keyboard, with a visible focus indicator that does not rely on colour alone.
- **Semantic structure.** Native HTML elements are used for their meaning — headings describe a real hierarchy, buttons are buttons, links are links.
- **Contrast.** Body text and interface text meet or exceed WCAG AA contrast ratios against their actual backgrounds.
- **Alternative text.** Every meaningful image carries descriptive alternative text. Decorative images are marked as such so assistive technology skips them rather than announcing noise.
- **Labels on every field.** Form fields have associated labels that make sense read out loud, and errors are explained in text rather than indicated by colour.
- **State changes announced.** Live regions announce streaming responses and status changes so a screen reader user is not left guessing whether anything happened.
- **Reduced motion respected.** `prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured, including for transitions and animated backgrounds.
- **No login or paywall wall.** Nothing about accessibility is conditional on an account.

## Performance

Performance work on this project follows a simple order of operations, most effective first:

1. **Ship less.** No analytics or advertising code on pages that do not need it.
2. **Render on the server.** Content is complete in the first response, so nothing is discovered by JavaScript afterwards.
3. **Cache at the edge.** Static assets and pages are served from cache close to the visitor.
4. **Defer what remains.** Third-party scripts load after the page is usable, never before.
5. **Resize images properly.** Images are served at the size they are displayed and in modern formats.
6. **Reserve space for dynamic content.** Layout does not jump when content arrives.

The measurable goals are a fast first render on mobile data, stable layout while responses stream, and no interaction that depends on a request completing.

## Privacy

Anime Array is built so that the privacy properties are structural rather than promised.

- **No user data is sold.** Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to anyone.
- **Advertising is the business model**, and it is labelled as such on every page that carries an ad.
- **Third-party scripts run server-side where possible**, so API credentials and advertising identifiers are not exposed in page source.
- **Accounts are optional.** You can use every core feature without providing an email address.
- **Analytics are used to understand how the site is used**, not to build a profile of individuals. Site administrators are excluded from reporting so internal traffic does not distort the figures.
- **No chat history is sold, shared, or used for advertising.**

Further detail is on the live site's [Privacy Policy](https://animearray.site/privacy-policy/).

## What we will never do

A short list, stated publicly so that nobody has to take it on trust:

- **Host or mirror video.** No episodes, films, subtitles, or media files. The site points people to legitimate platforms; it does not replace them.
- **Claim affiliation we do not have.** No studio, publisher, licensor, network or distributor has endorsed, licensed, sponsored or approved this project. Saying otherwise would be a lie with legal consequences.
- **Voice cloning.** No character's dialogue is spoken by a real person in real time, and no voice actor's performance is reproduced.
- **Sell or broker personal data.** Advertising pays for the site; it does not purchase anything about you.
- **Charge for a core feature.** A paywall behind something basic would contradict the entire premise.
- **Use dark patterns.** No fake download buttons, no fake play buttons, no countdown timers, no misleading "click here to download" prompts.

Corrections, copyright concerns and content questions are handled directly through the [contact form](https://animearray.site/contact-us/).

## Frequently asked questions

The full set is on the [FAQ page](docs/faq.html) and on the [live site's FAQ](https://animearray.site/faqs/). The five that matter most:

**Is Anime Array free?**
Yes. Every core feature is free with no account required, and every core feature still works with advertising blocked. The site is funded by clearly labelled advertising.

**Does Anime Array host anime or stream video?**
No. No episodes, films or media files are hosted, uploaded or mirrored. It is a discovery and conversation platform, and it links to nowhere you should not.

**Is Anime Array affiliated with any anime studio or publisher?**
No. It is an independent fan project with no endorsement, sponsorship or licence from any studio, publisher, licensor or rights holder. Character and series names are used only to identify the source material the characters are inspired by.

**Do the characters' replies always match canon?**
No, and deliberately so. The goal is a conversation that feels true to the character's temperament, not a script. Where canon does not cover a situation, the reply is reasoned from their personality rather than looked up — and it should not be read as a quotation.

**Are the characters real people, or does Anime Array clone voices?**
Neither. They are text-based AI systems inspired by anime characters. They are not the real individuals, not the voice actors, and no vocal performance is reproduced anywhere on the platform.

Corrections are the highest-priority kind of report this project receives, and making one needs no technical skill. See [Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md#1-corrections).

## The checks are the point

This repository verifies its own claims rather than asserting them. That is unusual for documentation, and it is deliberate, because most of what goes wrong in a content repository is small drift that nobody notices until it is quoted somewhere else.

**`tools/check-data.mjs` cross-validates `data/` against `docs/`.** If a character is added, renamed, removed, or has its temperament reworded, the build fails unless *every* page stating that fact is updated in the same commit. The same applies to the tool count, the mode count and the platform claims.

That rule exists because of a specific failure mode. In `guides/ai-visibility.md` this repository claims that a stale count is the most damaging error its content can contain — an assistant asked "how many characters?" will pick one of two contradicting numbers and there is no way to predict which. Claiming to prevent that without actually checking would be worse than not claiming it, so the check exists and the negative test passes:

```
$ node tools/check-data.mjs
- characters.html does not mention "Kakashi Hatake"
- index.html roster is missing "Kakashi Hatake"
- about.html never states the character total
- about.html states "twelve characters" but the roster is 13
```

**`tools/check-site.mjs` validates the published pages.** It checks the single `<h1>`, title and description lengths, canonical and social metadata, JSON-LD parsing and `@id` resolution, `alt` text, that every internal link resolves, that the sitemap matches the files on disk, that social cards exist and are not shared between content pages, that the header nav is identical everywhere with `aria-current` only on the current page, and that no page is orphaned.

**The leak guard.** CI greps the repository for internal paths, stack names and credential fragments. The terms are assembled from fragments at run time rather than written literally, because a checker that contained every term it forbids would publish exactly the list it exists to keep private.

### Why so much checking for a documentation site

Because this content is read by machines as well as people. Search crawlers, answer engines and anyone integrating against `data/` all take these statements at face value. A number that is wrong in two places is worse than a number that is missing.

## Getting the words in front of people

The documentation site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which search engines discount heavily. The writing is good; good writing on a discounted domain stays invisible. That is a property of the hosting, not of the content, and it means the site is not where search results come from.

[`PRESS-KIT.md`](PRESS-KIT.md) exists for that reason. It contains:

- **Boilerplate** at three lengths, ready to paste

Use them on your own domain, on guest posts, in community forums, in resource roundups. The documentation site remains the canonical source and is worth having as documentation and as a credibility signal. It is simply not the channel that will move rankings.
